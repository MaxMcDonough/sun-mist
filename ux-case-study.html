Project Overview

Sun Mist is an on-demand mobile sunscreen misting service for beaches. Unlike traditional application methods, Sun Mist operators equipped with spray devices move through beaches offering quick, convenient sunscreen application for beachgoers.

How might we make sun protection effortless, non-invasive, and even delightful for beachgoers?

Project Scope

This case study documents the complete UX research and service design process for Sun Mist, from initial discovery through pilot validation. The project investigated beach visitor behaviors, pain points with sun protection, and opportunities to deliver a high-quality service that integrates seamlessly into the beach experience.

Research Methodology

Our approach combined quantitative surveys (412 beach visitors) with qualitative field observations (28 hours), competitive analysis, usability testing (24 participants), and on-beach pilot testing (6 weeks). This mixed-methods approach ensured we understood both the breadth of user needs and the depth of contextual factors influencing behavior.

Qualitative Field Observations

We spent 28 hours observing beach behaviors across five different beaches, documenting sunscreen application patterns, pain points, and micro-moments where an intervention could occur.

The Reapplication Gap
Visitors apply sunscreen initially but almost never reapply mid-day. We observed that nearly 80% of visitors never reapply, despite being at the beach 5+ hours. The friction of stopping activities, finding sunscreen, and disrupting the beach experience is too high.
Parent Anxiety
Parents with young children experience constant worry about sun exposure. We documented 3-4 sunscreen application attempts per family, often resulting in conflict with children. Parents view sun protection as a persistent stressor rather than a solved problem.
Group Dynamics
One person applying sunscreen triggers others in the group to do the same. We observed peer influence drives behavior—friends or strangers at adjacent spots applying sunscreen creates a moment of vulnerability where groups acknowledge their sun protection needs.
The Convenience Premium
Visitors showed they will pay for convenience—we saw consistent purchases of premium items (beach service beverages at 4x markup, convenience retail at 2x). Surveys showed 67% willing to pay $5-8 for quick sunscreen application versus hassling with personal sunscreen.

Competitive Landscape

We analyzed competitors across the sun protection and beach service spaces to identify Sun Mist's positioning opportunity:

Competitive Positioning Matrix

Coverage Quality vs. Convenience

Competitor Positioning:

  • Traditional Sunscreen: High coverage, low convenience (requires self-application, messy)
  • Spray Booths: High convenience but moderate coverage (uneven application)
  • Beach Vendors: Moderate both (trusted source but inconsistent quality)
  • DIY Aerosol: Low convenience & coverage (poorly formulated, unhealthy)
  • Sun Mist: Positioned for high convenience AND professional-grade coverage quality

Key Research Insights

Five critical insights emerged to guide the design:

  1. The 20-second threshold: Anything requiring more than 20 seconds disrupts the beach experience. Speed is the primary value proposition.
  2. Autonomy matters: Beachgoers must maintain control—the service can't feel pushy or invasive. Customers initiate; operators facilitate.
  3. Transparency builds trust: Customers want to know exactly what product is being applied and why. Clear communication removes anxiety about untested products.
  4. Social proof accelerates adoption: When one person uses the service, others in proximity notice and become interested. Visible success drives organic growth.
  5. Repeat usage indicates value: Customers who use the service once and experience benefits are likely to seek it out again same-day. Second usage within 24 hours is our north star metric.

Design Solution

Based on research insights, we developed a service design centered on four core principles that guide every decision—from operator training to customer interaction to payment systems.

Service Design Principles

⚡
Frictionless
Complete service delivery in under 20 seconds. Every interface, process, and decision is optimized for speed without sacrificing quality.
🎯
Non-Invasive
Customer always maintains control. Operators approach respectfully, explain clearly, and accept "no" gracefully. The service fits into beach activities, not interrupts them.
👁️
Transparent
Clear visibility into what, why, and how much. Product displayed, benefits explained, pricing stated upfront. Trust is earned through openness.
😊
Delightful
A moment of joy in a beach day. Operators are genuinely friendly. The spray application should feel good. Payment is easy. Customers leave smiling.

Key Iterations Based on Testing

❌ Initial Approach Script

"Would you be interested in professional sunscreen application? It's only $6 and takes 20 seconds."

Feedback: Felt salesy. People associated "professional" with formal/invasive.

✅ Revised Approach Script

"Quick question—have you reapplied sunscreen since this morning? We offer a quick spray if you'd like."

Impact: +23% initiation rate. Feels helpful, not pushy. Acknowledges their current behavior.

❌ Product Presentation

Kept sunscreen bottle in bag. Only showed when asked. Some customers wondered what product was being sprayed.

Feedback: Lack of visibility created trust concerns. Customers wanted to see the product.

✅ Product Presentation (Revised)

Large, visible branded bottle holder. Always show product and SPF label during approach. Emphasize "dermatologist-approved."

Impact: +31% confidence in product. Trust increased immediately with visible label.
